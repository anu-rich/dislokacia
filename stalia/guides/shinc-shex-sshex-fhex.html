SHINC, SHEX, SSHEX, FHEX: which days count as laytime

A few letters in the laytime clause decide whether the weekend costs the charterer time. Here is what each term means and how much it changes one and the same port call.

The terms

SHINC
Sundays and holidays included. Every day counts, weekends and holidays too. Usual for tankers and for terminals that work around the clock.
SHEX
Sundays and holidays excepted. Sundays and official holidays do not count. Saturday counts in full unless the clause says otherwise.
SSHEX
Saturdays, Sundays and holidays excepted. The whole weekend is outside laytime.
FHEX
Fridays and holidays excepted. Used where Friday is the day of rest. Where the weekend is Friday and Saturday, the charter party should say so in words.
Saturday 12:00 to Monday 08:00
A common amendment: the excepted period is fixed by clock times instead of calendar days. Friday 17:00 to Monday 08:00 is another.
UU — unless used
If cargo is worked during an excepted period, the time actually used counts.
EIU — even if used
An excepted period never counts, whether or not cargo is worked.
WWD — weather working day
Time lost to bad weather does not count. It is entered as an interruption from the statement of facts.

One port call, six results

Take 72 hours of laytime allowed. Laytime starts on Friday at 13:00 and loading is completed on the following Tuesday at 12:00: 95 hours on the calendar, with no weather or other interruptions. Demurrage is USD 12,000 per day, despatch half of that.

ClauseLaytime usedResultAmount
SHINC72 h, expired Mon 13:0023 h on demurrageUSD 11,500 demurrage
FHEX72 h, expired Tue 00:0012 h on demurrageUSD 6,000 demurrage
SHEX71 h1 h savedUSD 250 despatch
SSHEX, unless used, 12 h worked on Saturday59 h13 h savedUSD 3,250 despatch
Saturday 12:00 to Monday 08:00 excepted51 h21 h savedUSD 5,250 despatch
SSHEX47 h25 h savedUSD 6,250 despatch

Between the first line and the last, nothing changed in the port. The clause alone moved the result by USD 17,750.

Holidays

Under SHEX, SSHEX and FHEX, holidays are excepted in the same way as the weekly day of rest. Three questions decide how many hours that is:

  • Which holidays. Official holidays at the port, national or local, as the charter party defines them. A holiday that falls on a day already excepted adds nothing.
  • From when to when. Many clauses run the excepted period from 17:00 on the day before the holiday until 08:00 on the day after. That turns a 24-hour holiday into 39 hours.
  • Whether work counts. “Unless used” and “even if used” apply to holidays exactly as they do to weekends.

Under SHINC, holidays count like any other day.

After laytime has expired

The excepted periods protect the charterer only while laytime is running. Once it has expired, the vessel is on demurrage and, unless the charter party says otherwise, time runs through weekends and holidays without a break.

The effect is large. With only 24 hours allowed under SHEX in the example above, laytime runs out on Saturday at 13:00. The Sunday that follows counts in full: 71 hours on demurrage. If the charter party applied its exceptions to demurrage as well, the same Sunday would be free and the figure would be 47 hours.

What to check in your charter party

  • Is Saturday in or out, and from what hour?
  • Is it “unless used” or “even if used”? If neither is written, do not assume.
  • Do holidays start on the eve and end the next morning?
  • Do the exceptions apply once the vessel is on demurrage?

These terms are shorthand, and charter parties amend them freely. The wording in your charter party always comes first.
